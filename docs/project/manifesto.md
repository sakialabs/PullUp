# The PullUp Manifesto

> Your people. Your place. PullUp.

**Version:** 1.1  
**Status:** Public-benefit commitments baseline  
**Date:** August 24, 2026

## Housing is where life begins

A home is not a stock chip with a kitchen.

It is where people sleep after a shift, raise children, recover from illness, build relationships, study, make art, care for elders, plan a future, and sometimes simply close the door and breathe.

When housing is insecure, every other part of life pays the price.

PullUp begins with a direct belief:

**Housing is a human right and social infrastructure. It should not be organized mainly to maximize extraction from people who need somewhere to live.**

## We refuse the fake choice

We reject the idea that people must choose between:

- a cold public bureaucracy and a polished corporate landlord;
- insecure renting and lifelong debt;
- privacy and useful technology;
- affordability and beauty;
- personal space and community;
- climate responsibility and a comfortable life;
- local control and modern infrastructure.

These are design and political choices, not laws of nature.

## Working people already create the value

Workers build the homes, staff the hospitals, teach the children, run the transit, grow the food, make the culture, maintain the city, care for families, and pay taxes.

Then many are asked to surrender an ever-growing share of their income because the land beneath daily life became an investment vehicle.

People should not have to pay twice:

1. once through labour, taxes, care, and community-building;
2. again through speculative land extraction.

Public investment and community effort should create permanent community wealth.

## Housing must leave the casino

Software alone cannot end landlord or corporate greed.

Greed survives a nicer interface when ownership, finance, law, and power remain unchanged.

PullUp therefore joins technology to material structures:

- public and community land;
- cooperative and nonprofit ownership;
- long-term use rights;
- cost-based housing charges;
- secure tenure;
- strong reserves;
- anti-speculation mission locks;
- democratic resident power;
- patient, mission-aligned capital;
- public policy and organizing.

The app is the connective tissue. It is not the revolution in a rounded rectangle.

## Permanent means permanent

If the public provides land, grants, loans, infrastructure, or tax support, the resulting affordability should not disappear at the first attractive sale.

If residents build value through mutual aid, governance, stewardship, or payment, that value should not become a private landlord’s exit.

PullUp supports legal and financial structures that keep homes outside speculation across generations.

The next resident deserves the same protection as the first.

## Democracy belongs at home

People spend huge portions of their income on housing but often have almost no say over the systems that govern it.

PullUp brings democratic practice into:

- budgets;
- shared spaces;
- services;
- repairs;
- energy;
- community assets;
- local leadership;
- provider accountability;
- future development.

One vote is not enough if information is unreadable, meetings are inaccessible, work is unpaid, or power already decided the answer.

Real democracy needs:

- plain information;
- translation;
- disability access;
- online and offline participation;
- time to deliberate;
- paid substantial resident work;
- conflict disclosure;
- transparent records;
- reasons;
- appeal;
- protected rights that a majority cannot erase.

## Solidarity is not forced friendship

Community can be beautiful. It can also become exhausting when institutions use it to replace paid services.

PullUp invites people to share tools, rooms, skills, care, events, energy, and action. It does not demand constant social performance.

No one must:

- volunteer to keep their home;
- join fLOKr;
- attend events;
- share private life;
- become friends with neighbours;
- use Day One;
- earn a reputation score;
- prove they are “community-minded” enough to deserve housing.

People may connect deeply or live quietly. Both belong.

## A subscription must serve the member

Modern subscription models can pool demand, make costs predictable, maintain assets, and provide portable services.

They can also hide fees, trap people, divide users into tiers, and charge forever for basic access.

PullUp takes the useful mechanics and rejects subscription hell.

Essential housing rights are not add-ons.

There is no gold tier for:

- timely repairs;
- safer equipment;
- due process;
- privacy;
- accessibility;
- queue priority;
- secure tenure.

Optional services must reflect genuine additional cost. Cancellation must be simple. No free-trial traps, loyalty penalties, fake bundles, or late-fee profit.

## The full cost belongs in daylight

Advertised rent is often the opening line, not the real monthly damage.

PullUp shows **Total Damage**:

- housing charge;
- utilities;
- required fees;
- transport context;
- deposits or member contribution;
- insurance;
- move-in costs;
- real optional extras.

**Where It Goes** explains what each charge funds and who receives it.

If a housing model cannot survive transparent math, the problem is not the label.

## Technology should do paperwork, not rule people

Good technology can:

- make housing information clear;
- reduce repeated forms;
- translate;
- route repairs;
- forecast maintenance;
- coordinate shared assets;
- explain documents;
- make budgets understandable;
- widen participation;
- improve energy systems;
- expose unfair patterns.

It should not decide who is worthy of a home.

PullUp rejects:

- universal tenant scores;
- behavioural and vulnerability scores;
- automated rejection;
- eviction recommendations;
- dynamic housing prices;
- face-first roommate ranking;
- protected-trait inference;
- social popularity as eligibility;
- surveillance dressed as convenience.

Consequential decisions require rules people can inspect, reasons they can understand, human review, appeal, and fairness testing.

## Intelligence can be quiet

People do not need a fake AI friend popping up between them and every serious task.

PullUp uses intelligence subtly for recommendations, translation, summaries, forecasting, anomaly detection, routing, and fairness monitoring.

There is no required mascot.

When someone needs support, **Need a Hand?** connects them to the right explanation, flow, Twin, worker, legal source, or emergency path.

The best automation often feels like less admin, not more personality.

## Data is not rent

People already pay for housing. They should not also pay with an intimate map of their lives.

PullUp treats privacy as part of the home.

Residents control application sharing through Home Passport. They control permissions through My Business. They see who accessed what and when.

PullUp does not sell resident data. It does not use household behaviour to set price, rank worth, target ads, discipline people, or recommend eviction.

Portability matters too. No community should become trapped because a vendor holds its records hostage.

## A smart home must not become a surveillance landlord

Sensors can prevent floods, improve air, identify failing equipment, and reduce energy bills.

They can also reveal intimate patterns.

PullUp welcomes useful, resident-respecting technology and draws a hard line:

- no cameras or microphones inside homes;
- no facial, voice, gait, or emotion recognition;
- no private-room occupancy inference;
- no device data for eligibility, pricing, punishment, or eviction;
- no secret reuse or sale;
- no remote lockout as discipline;
- always a physical or human fallback.

Every device must answer:

1. What problem does this solve?
2. What does it sense?
3. Who sees the data?
4. How long is it kept?
5. What happens when it fails?
6. Can a person still live safely without the app?

## Solarpunk is a practice

Solarpunk is not green gradients and plants on a landlord dashboard.

It means building a life that is low-carbon, beautiful, repairable, shared where useful, and democratically governed.

PullUp chooses:

- repair before replacement;
- retrofit before demolition where safe and feasible;
- passive design before gadget excess;
- healthy air and water;
- efficient electric systems;
- renewable energy and storage;
- gardens and biodiversity;
- local skills and materials;
- comfort and lower bills;
- resilience during heat, storms, outages, and disruption.

Climate action that makes working people colder, poorer, or more surveilled is not climate justice.

## The library economy is practical abundance

Millions of people each buy tools, appliances, vehicles, rooms, and equipment that sit unused most of the time.

The PullUp Commons turns idle capacity into shared access:

- tools;
- cargo bikes;
- guest rooms;
- studios;
- work rooms;
- kitchen equipment;
- accessibility equipment;
- gardens;
- storage;
- books and games;
- skills;
- locally generated energy.

This is not about owning nothing and being grateful.

It is about spending less for access to more, while reducing waste and freeing space.

Personal, intimate, and frequently used things can remain personal. Shared ownership is a choice where it creates real abundance.

## Day One is chosen trust, powered up

Not everyone wants to face housing alone.

Day One is the shared human-support layer across Sakia Labs. A Twin is the one person the user chooses inside it: a friend, sibling, partner, parent, roommate, neighbour, advocate, or chosen family member.

Together they can compare Places, join a tour, review a selected document, Tag Team tasks, build Our Move, split approved costs, confirm a sensitive action, share a check-in, or ask each other to Pull Through.

Trust is never inferred. Access is never broad by default. Max Pass makes permissions granular, visible, expiring, and revocable. Receipts makes activity inspectable.

Day One makes technology more human by strengthening a real relationship, not replacing one. It is a core capability, not a premium subscription.

## fLOKr is community, not credit

fLOKr helps people find Circles, post Signals, form Crews, discover Shifts, and make Moves.

PullUp connects residents to that community layer.

But likes, attendance, volunteer hours, popularity, disagreements, and social visibility never enter housing decisions.

Community is a right and an opportunity, not a credit score.

## We learn globally and govern locally

PullUp learns from:

- Vienna’s public land, patient finance, limited-profit housing, and renovation;
- Finland’s housing-first dignity;
- Uruguay’s mutual aid, self-management, and technical accompaniment;
- community land trusts;
- Germany’s anti-privatization housing network;
- Catalonia’s right-to-use cooperatives;
- Canadian housing co-ops;
- cooperative energy;
- democratic digital infrastructure.

We copy mechanisms, not mythology.

Every model carries history, law, culture, strengths, and limits. PullUp must adapt locally with residents, housing providers, workers, legal experts, and communities.

Indigenous land, rights, and governance cannot be absorbed into a generic “community ownership” brand. Partnership must be invited, specific, and accountable.

## We scale by federation, not empire

The goal is not for Sakia Labs to own every home and become the landlord it set out to defeat.

Local communities should own and govern local housing.

PullUp can provide:

- shared software;
- open protocols;
- training;
- technical assistance;
- group purchasing;
- service standards;
- learning;
- portable resident tools;
- mission-lock support.

Scale should increase bargaining power and capability while keeping ownership close to the people who live with the consequences.

## We measure freedom, not engagement

PullUp is successful when:

- Total Damage falls;
- homes remain affordable;
- people stay housed;
- repairs improve;
- applications become less invasive;
- residents understand decisions;
- public investment stays public in purpose;
- more assets are shared;
- energy bills and emissions fall together;
- resident power grows;
- people recover time for life.

Screen time, swipe count, and notification opens are not the mission.

The ultimate metric is simple:

**Do working people have more security, more time, more dignity, more control, and a healthier planet because PullUp exists?**

## Our commitments

We commit to:

1. design with residents and pay for substantial resident expertise;
2. publish material rules and conflicts;
3. keep essential rights outside premium tiers;
4. refuse revenue tied to rent hikes, evictions, ads, data sales, or speculative resale;
5. protect affordability through ownership and legal structure;
6. keep AI away from autonomous housing judgment;
7. provide human review and appeal;
8. build accessible online and offline routes;
9. minimize data and make permissions understandable;
10. use IoT only for clear resident and building benefit;
11. measure disparate outcomes and pause harmful systems;
12. keep local governance strong as the network grows;
13. direct surplus to homes, reserves, retrofits, services, and future affordability;
14. tell the truth about limits;
15. remember that software is a tool inside a social movement, not the movement itself.

## The invitation

To residents: this should feel like your infrastructure.

To workers: your labour should buy more life, not only more rent.

To housing providers: modern operations and democratic accountability can strengthen each other.

To cities: public land and investment can build permanent affordability.

To cooperatives and organizers: PullUp should amplify existing power, not rebrand it.

To technologists: automate the burden, expose the rules, protect the person.

To investors and funders: patient capital can create durable public value without extracting the future.

To everyone building the next world:

**Find your fLOKr. PullUp to your place. TapIn.**

We are not building a more addictive way to browse rent.

We are building a calmer way to find a home, a fairer way to pay for it, a democratic way to run it, a practical way to share abundance, and a durable way to keep it out of the casino.

**Homes made for chillin’ not flipping.**
