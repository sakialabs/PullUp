# PullUp Foundation

> Homes made for chillin’ not flipping.

**Version:** 1.1  
**Status:** Product foundation baseline  
**Project:** PullUp by Sakia Labs  
**Date:** August 2026

## 1. The idea

PullUp is a public-benefit housing platform and membership network for finding, entering, sharing, running, and protecting a home.

It combines a warm consumer experience with serious housing infrastructure. A person can discover a Place, understand the Total Damage, apply once with a reusable Home Passport, Pull Up for a tour, move in, report Fixes, borrow from the Commons, participate through fLOKr, and use Day One to move with one trusted person.

The interface should feel as easy as a modern social app. The institution underneath must behave more like a cooperative, community land trust, public utility, and library working together.

PullUp is not a prettier rental marketplace. A marketplace can reduce search friction while leaving speculation, rent extraction, weak tenant power, and insecure tenure untouched. PullUp is designed to change the rules underneath the screen.

## 2. Mission

Make safe, stable, climate-ready homes easier to access and harder to exploit.

PullUp uses community ownership, cost-based housing, democratic governance, shared infrastructure, and humane technology to return time, money, security, and power to ordinary people.

## 3. Vision

A world where:

- a home is treated as social infrastructure and a human right;
- land and buildings supported by public or community effort stay affordable across generations;
- residents have real power over budgets, rules, services, and shared spaces;
- housing costs reflect the cost of providing a good home, not the maximum distress a market can absorb;
- energy, tools, rooms, mobility, knowledge, and care can be shared without forcing anyone into compulsory community;
- technology handles paperwork and coordination quietly, while people keep authority over consequential decisions;
- local PullUp networks can cooperate globally without becoming a remote corporate landlord.

## 4. The promise

### To residents

No hidden housing math. No popularity contest. No surveillance landlord. No premium tier for basic dignity.

### To communities

Public investment and community labour create permanent community wealth, not a future private windfall.

### To partners

PullUp provides shared digital operations, procurement, resident services, and learning without taking ownership away from local people.

### To the planet

Repair first. Retrofit deeply. Share more. Waste less. Make clean energy a community asset.

## 5. The problem PullUp is built to solve

Housing failure is not one bug.

It is a stack of connected failures:

1. **Land and homes are treated as financial assets.** Scarcity and insecurity become revenue opportunities.
2. **Supply is too slow, mismatched, or unaffordable.** Canada needs much more housing, but unit count alone does not guarantee affordability, accessibility, or secure tenure.
3. **The full cost is hidden.** Rent is advertised without utilities, transport, deposits, fees, furniture, energy quality, or likely repair burden.
4. **Applying is repetitive and invasive.** People repeatedly surrender sensitive documents to unknown actors.
5. **Power is unequal after move-in.** Repairs, renewals, transfers, accessibility requests, and disputes are difficult to track.
6. **People pay separately for assets that sit idle.** Tools, guest rooms, mobility, appliances, storage, energy, and knowledge are duplicated.
7. **Housing technology often serves extraction.** Screening scores, dynamic pricing, paid listing boosts, surveillance, and automated rejection make old power imbalances faster.
8. **Climate risk lands on residents.** Poor insulation, heat, leaks, bad air, energy volatility, and weak maintenance cost households money and health.
9. **Community participation is often fake or exhausting.** A survey is not democracy, and unpaid meetings cannot become a second job.

PullUp treats these as one system. Search, tenure, ownership, money, governance, maintenance, energy, data, and community must reinforce each other.

## 6. The constitutional principles

These rules govern the product, operating model, and future technical design.

### 6.1 Housing before engagement

Housing eligibility, price, repairs, rights, and tenure never depend on social activity, app use, reputation, volunteer hours, fLOKr participation, or using Day One.

### 6.2 Permanent affordability

Public land, grants, community labour, and resident payments must not produce a private speculative exit later. Land and buildings use mission locks, collective ownership, long leases, or equivalent legal controls.

### 6.3 Cost before market maximum

Charges are based on real operating costs, reserves, debt, utilities, services, and agreed community investment. PullUp does not ask, “What can we get away with?”

### 6.4 Secure tenure

A membership is not a disposable hotel booking. Residents need clear agreements, predictable costs, due process, repair rights, human review, and meaningful paths to Stay Put or Switch Up.

### 6.5 One class of dignity

There is no premium resident tier for faster repairs, safer homes, better rights, queue priority, or access to essential services.

### 6.6 Democracy with teeth

Residents have defined voting rights, accessible participation, transparent budgets, conflict disclosures, appeal paths, and protected authority over material decisions.

### 6.7 Privacy is part of the home

The home is not a data mine. PullUp collects the minimum information needed, gives people understandable controls, and never sells resident data.

### 6.8 Technology is support, not government

Software may recommend, translate, detect, forecast, route, or summarize. It does not silently decide who deserves a home, a repair, a lower price, or continued tenure.

### 6.9 Access without performance

Swipes are optional. Every important action also works with buttons, keyboard, screen reader, list, map, phone, print, kiosk, or human assistance.

### 6.10 Community without coercion

People can share, organize, celebrate, or keep to themselves. No one owes unpaid emotional labour, public visibility, or constant neighbourliness in exchange for housing.

### 6.11 Local ownership, shared infrastructure

Homes stay locally governed. Common protocols, software, training, finance patterns, and purchasing power can be federated across PullUp networks.

### 6.12 Climate justice, not gadget theatre

Energy and IoT features must reduce bills, emissions, risk, or maintenance burden. A connected toaster with a data plan is not liberation.

## 7. What the world already teaches us

PullUp does not invent housing justice from scratch. It combines durable mechanisms from places and movements that have already done meaningful work.

| Model | What works | PullUp adaptation | What not to copy blindly |
|---|---|---|---|
| Red Vienna and modern Vienna | Public land, municipal capacity, long-term subsidy, limited-profit delivery, renovation, and quality competition | Land strategy, patient finance, cost discipline, mixed-income housing, and four-part quality review covering economy, ecology, social value, and design | A city-specific bureaucracy cannot simply be pasted into every legal system |
| Finland’s housing-first approach | Stable housing is the starting point for solving homelessness, paired with support that does not make housing conditional on “readiness” | Stay Put, housing stability support, optional human services, and housing before engagement | PullUp is not a substitute for public housing, healthcare, or income policy |
| Uruguay’s FUCVAM mutual-aid cooperatives | Collective ownership, self-management, democratic participation, mutual aid, and professional technical assistance | Resident power plus paid technical, legal, financial, architectural, and organizing support | Mutual aid must not become forced or unpaid labour for people without time, health, or capacity |
| Community land trusts | Land is held for community benefit while residents receive secure, affordable use or ownership rights | Separate land from speculation and preserve affordability through resale or use controls | Stewardship needs stable funding and accountable governance |
| Mietshäuser Syndikat | Local house autonomy combined with a network-level veto against resale and privatization | A protected mission veto and solidarity contribution across local PullUp Places | Legal structure must be adapted locally and reviewed by counsel |
| Catalonia’s right-to-use cooperatives | The cooperative owns homes permanently; members receive long-term use rights and pay cost-linked charges | Secure membership, collective property, non-speculative use rights, and public land partnerships | High initial contributions can exclude working-class households unless financed or subsidized |
| Canadian housing co-ops | Member control, non-profit operation, community life, and established legal forms | A practical Canadian pilot base and familiar resident governance | Old buildings, deferred maintenance, waitlists, and weak operating tools still need investment |
| Singapore’s HDB system | Public land, state capacity, integrated neighbourhood planning, and large-scale housing delivery | Coordinate housing with transit, services, public space, and long-term asset management | PullUp rejects coercive, exclusionary, or surveillance-heavy adaptations |
| Cooperative energy communities | People and local organizations can jointly produce, share, and benefit from renewable energy | Solar, batteries, heat pumps, charging, demand response, and savings governed as community infrastructure | Energy participation must be voluntary, understandable, and compatible with local law |
| Digital public infrastructure | Shared digital rails can support identity, payments, and data exchange when rights and safeguards are built in | Portable Home Passport, scoped permissions, open standards, and interoperable public-benefit services | Centralized identity or data exchange without strong safeguards can magnify harm |
| Decidim and cooperative principles | Digital participation can support transparent, democratic organizing | Accessible proposals, budgets, voting, records, and delegation paired with offline governance | Clicking a poll is not a replacement for organizing, deliberation, or legal power |

The synthesis is simple:

**Vienna brings public capacity. Uruguay brings mutual aid and technical accompaniment. Community land trusts and the Syndikat bring anti-speculation locks. Cooperatives bring resident control. Finland brings housing-first dignity. Solarpunk and the library economy bring shared abundance. PullUp connects the pieces without pretending an app created them.**

## 8. The institutional model

PullUp works best as a federation of locally owned housing networks with shared public-benefit infrastructure.

### 8.1 The land layer

A community land trust, municipality, Indigenous land partner where invited and appropriate, public agency, or aligned nonprofit holds land or grants a long-term ground lease.

Purpose:

- remove land from speculative resale;
- lower acquisition risk;
- preserve community control;
- support permanent affordability;
- recognize that local land relationships and Indigenous rights require specific, respectful governance rather than a generic template.

### 8.2 The home layer

A resident housing cooperative, nonprofit housing provider, public housing body, or mission-locked entity owns or operates each building.

Purpose:

- provide secure homes;
- maintain buildings;
- set cost-based charges;
- hold adequate reserves;
- protect due process;
- give residents real governance rights.

### 8.3 The network layer

A service cooperative or public-benefit operator runs PullUp’s shared platform, training, support, procurement, implementation, and learning network.

Purpose:

- reduce duplicated administration;
- pool demand for insurance, maintenance, energy, devices, and services;
- maintain open, interoperable product standards;
- support small providers that cannot build full digital operations alone;
- help new local networks launch without handing them to a corporate landlord.

### 8.4 The mission lock

Material sale, conversion, demutualization, or removal of affordability controls requires protected approval from the community-interest body, not only a temporary resident majority or investor.

A local Place keeps operational autonomy. The network holds a narrow veto over actions that would return the asset to speculation.

## 9. The money model

PullUp must be financially feasible without earning more when residents suffer.

### 9.1 Housing charges

Monthly housing charges may include:

- debt service;
- operations and staffing;
- maintenance;
- utilities where pooled;
- insurance and taxes;
- replacement reserves;
- accessibility and climate retrofit reserves;
- agreed contributions to future permanently affordable homes.

The calculation is visible in **Where It Goes**. The complete expected amount is **Total Damage**.

### 9.2 Capital stack

Projects may combine:

- public land or long ground leases;
- government grants and low-cost loans;
- credit unions and cooperative finance;
- community bonds;
- mission-aligned pension or institutional capital;
- philanthropic first-loss or predevelopment capital;
- resident contributions scaled to means;
- cross-subsidy within mixed-income portfolios;
- energy and retrofit incentives.

Capital must accept the mission lock. A low-cost loan is useful. A quiet claim on future rent extraction is not.

### 9.3 Surplus

After operating costs, debt, and reserves, surplus stays in the system for:

- deeper affordability;
- repairs and preventive maintenance;
- accessibility;
- climate retrofits;
- resident-approved services;
- technical support;
- new permanently affordable homes.

It is not distributed according to who can force the highest housing charge.

### 9.4 PullUp revenue

The platform can earn sustainable revenue through:

- per-home software and service fees paid by providers;
- implementation and migration support;
- training and technical assistance;
- shared procurement administration;
- facility and energy program support;
- public contracts and grants for public-benefit functions;
- optional resident services priced at genuine incremental cost.

PullUp does not earn from:

- rent spread or rent hikes;
- eviction volume;
- application or screening fees;
- sale of resident data;
- ads or paid listing boosts;
- hidden service fees;
- dynamic housing prices;
- speculative appreciation.

## 10. Subscription without subscription hell

PullUp can use modern membership economics without turning shelter into software-as-a-service.

### The useful parts

- predictable pooled costs;
- one reusable membership across participating Places;
- shared procurement;
- portable services;
- preventive maintenance;
- access to Commons;
- one support relationship instead of ten disconnected vendors.

### The boundary

Tenure and essential rights are not cancellable add-ons.

The base resident relationship includes a safe home, repairs, privacy, due process, accessibility, clear charges, and participation rights. Optional memberships may cover genuine extras such as expanded mobility, bookable studios, special equipment, or additional storage. Declining an extra never weakens the home.

There are no free-trial traps, cancellation mazes, loyalty penalties, artificial bundles, or premium queue access.

## 11. The library economy

Ownership is useful for personal and intimate things. It is wasteful for many expensive assets used a few hours a month.

PullUp Commons may include:

- tools and repair equipment;
- cargo bikes and mobility devices;
- guest rooms;
- work rooms, studios, and maker spaces;
- large kitchen equipment;
- laundry and cleaning equipment;
- gardens and food-growing space;
- storage;
- books, games, and media;
- accessibility equipment;
- skills, classes, and time-banked help where lawful and genuinely voluntary;
- locally generated energy and battery capacity.

The goal is not austerity dressed as sharing. The goal is access to more useful things with less personal cost, waste, storage, and ecological damage.

## 12. The solarpunk operating model

Solarpunk is not a visual theme pasted over a property app. It is a practical commitment to ecological repair, shared capability, beauty, and democratic control.

PullUp prioritizes:

1. maintenance before failure;
2. repair before replacement;
3. retrofit and adaptive reuse before demolition where safe and feasible;
4. passive design before extra machinery;
5. efficient electric systems;
6. renewable generation and storage;
7. shared assets before needless duplication;
8. local ecological knowledge and biodiversity;
9. healthy air, water, temperature, light, and materials;
10. resident savings and comfort as core climate outcomes.

Energy data is used to improve systems and share benefits. It is not used to infer private behaviour, shame households, or punish residents.

## 13. Democratic governance

### 13.1 Rights

Residents receive:

- clear membership and tenure documents;
- one-member-one-vote or an equivalent democratic right appropriate to the legal model;
- understandable budgets and service standards;
- proposal and voting rights on defined matters;
- access to records, with privacy protections;
- conflict disclosure;
- reasons for consequential decisions;
- human review and appeal;
- accessible, translated, online, phone, paper, and in-person participation.

### 13.2 Scope

Residents can govern matters such as:

- shared-space use;
- service priorities;
- annual operating choices;
- reserve and retrofit priorities;
- Commons rules;
- community events;
- elected leadership;
- local provider performance.

Some duties remain constrained by law, safety, financing covenants, human rights, and the mission lock. Democracy does not permit discrimination, unsafe buildings, or privatization of community assets.

### 13.3 Labour

Substantial governance, facilitation, maintenance, care, moderation, and technical work should be paid. Volunteerism can add joy. It cannot become the hidden subsidy that keeps the model alive.

### 13.4 Participation design

PullUp supports delegation, asynchronous input, meeting summaries, accessible proposals, budget views, and clear decision records. Important choices still require real deliberation and lawful processes.

## 14. The Sakia Labs ecosystem

PullUp is the housing layer in a broader public-benefit workflow.

| Product | Role | Connection to PullUp | Hard boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| **fLOKr** | Community and collective action | Residents find Circles, respond to Signals, join Crews, discover Shifts, and make Moves around local life | Social participation never affects housing access, price, repairs, or tenure |
| **TapIn** | Shared action rail | Handles approved payments, bookings, joining, reservations, confirmations, shared costs, and temporary access | No hidden credit scoring, default financial access for a Twin, or remote punishment lockouts |
| **Lexi** | Plain-language rights and documents | Explains Fine Print, deadlines, policies, sources, and options | Lexi does not make final legal decisions or replace qualified counsel |
| **Makana** | Private life transitions and wellbeing | Supports moving, routines, goals, and LifeMaxxxin around a stable home | Private reflections never enter housing decisions |
| **Day One** | Shared human-support layer | One chosen Twin can review, plan, introduce, coordinate, approve, check in, or help with selected tasks across Sakia Labs | Access is granular, visible, expiring, revocable, and never assumed |

The shared workflow is:

**Find your fLOKr. PullUp to your place. TapIn.**

## 15. Day One as human infrastructure

Day One is the shared human-support layer across Sakia Labs. It is not an AI clone, a roommate score, an algorithmic soulmate, or a premium tier.

The trusted person inside Day One is a **Twin**. They may be a friend, sibling, partner, parent, roommate, neighbour, advocate, or chosen family member.

Day One supports more than backup:

| Move | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Twin Up** | Create the trusted connection |
| **Twin Mode** | Collaborate live on selected work |
| **Tag Team** | Share tasks, dates, notes, and responsibilities |
| **Run It By Me** | Request a second opinion before deciding |
| **Put Me On** | Share selected Places, people, opportunities, Circles, or Leads |
| **Hold It Down** | Delegate specific tasks for a limited time |
| **Double Tap** | Require both people to confirm a sensitive action |
| **Split It** | Divide an approved cost without exposing full accounts |
| **Pull Through** | Ask for urgent practical help |
| **Check In** | Share a chosen wellbeing, safety, or progress signal |
| **Our Move** | Build one shared plan |
| **Max Pass** | Grant narrow access that expires automatically |
| **Receipts** | Review exactly what each person viewed, changed, or approved |

In PullUp, a Twin may compare selected Places, join a tour, review chosen Home Passport items, help with a Move Plan, track a selected Fix, or use an explicitly approved TapIn action.

Permissions can expire. They can be revoked at any time. Access is logged. Private Makana reflections, general finances, permanent keys, and unrelated activity are never shared by default.

Day One expands what trusted people can do together without expanding what platforms can take from them.

## 16. Quiet intelligence

PullUp should feel smart without placing a fake assistant between people and their homes.

There is no named AI mascot and no compulsory chatbot.

Data science and AI may support:

- better Place recommendations;
- plain-language summaries;
- translation;
- accessible formatting;
- duplicate and fraud detection;
- maintenance routing;
- repair-risk and stock forecasting;
- energy optimization;
- wait-time estimates;
- anomaly detection;
- fairness monitoring.

Consequential decisions require explicit rules, reasons, human review, appeal, and disparity testing. PullUp prohibits:

- tenant, vulnerability, behaviour, or social scores;
- face-first or protected-trait inference;
- automated housing rejection;
- eviction recommendations;
- dynamic rent;
- social participation scoring;
- manipulative urgency;
- secret ranking sold to providers.

## 17. Data and interoperability

### 17.1 Resident control

**My Business** is the resident’s privacy and permission centre.

People can see:

- what data exists;
- why it is needed;
- who can access it;
- what was shared;
- when access expires;
- how to correct, export, or withdraw information where permitted.

### 17.2 Data classes

PullUp separates:

- public Place information;
- resident-controlled application data;
- household operations;
- building maintenance data;
- governance records;
- sensitive support information;
- anonymized and aggregated system learning.

Access to one class never silently grants access to another.

### 17.3 Open public-interest rails

PullUp should use exportable data, documented interfaces, open standards, and portable identity and consent patterns. Communities must be able to leave a service provider without losing their records or becoming digitally homeless.

## 18. IoT without a surveillance landlord

Connected systems can prevent damage, improve comfort, and lower costs. They can also expose intimate life patterns.

Allowed uses may include:

- leak and flood detection;
- indoor air-quality and temperature monitoring in resident-approved locations;
- equipment health;
- energy generation, storage, and demand response;
- shared appliance availability;
- secure, time-limited access;
- maintenance tags and manuals;
- accessibility controls.

PullUp does not use:

- cameras or microphones inside homes;
- facial, gait, voice, or emotion recognition;
- private-room occupancy inference;
- behavioural monitoring;
- device data for eligibility, pricing, discipline, or eviction;
- silent sale or cross-platform reuse of sensor data;
- remote lockout as punishment.

Devices should be local-first where practical, clearly marked, documented, replaceable, secure, and paired with physical or offline fallbacks.

## 19. A universal interface

PullUp’s Flow can be playful without becoming exclusionary.

### Optional gesture layer

- swipe right: **Keep**
- swipe left: **Not It**
- swipe up: **Pull Up**, meaning book or request a tour

### Equal alternatives

Every action is available through visible buttons and also works through:

- keyboard;
- screen reader;
- voice and switch access;
- list and map views;
- phone;
- paper;
- kiosk;
- trusted human assistance.

Place cards lead with Total Damage, tenure, availability, accessibility, and essential fit before glossy imagery. WCAG 2.2 is a floor, not the full definition of dignity.

People may swipe on Places. PullUp never swipes on people.

## 20. Local replication

PullUp scales by federation, not by owning every building from a distant headquarters.

Each local network adapts:

- legal form;
- land partnerships;
- tenant and cooperative law;
- income rules;
- accessibility standards;
- language;
- cultural practice;
- climate design;
- support partnerships.

The shared layer standardizes only what creates public value:

- affordability and mission-lock principles;
- resident rights;
- privacy and AI rules;
- interoperable records;
- service-quality measures;
- product patterns;
- training;
- procurement;
- transparent learning.

## 21. First pilot direction

The recommended first proof is one tightly supported partnership in London, Ontario.

### Shape

- one established nonprofit or housing co-op partner;
- roughly 50 to 150 existing homes;
- a paid resident advisory group;
- legal, privacy, accessibility, and human-rights review;
- PullUp discovery and resident operations where relevant;
- Fixes, Commons, The Board, and fLOKr connection;
- a small, consent-based sensor program for leaks, air, or equipment;
- clear baseline and outcome measurement.

### Why existing homes first

New construction matters, but software can prove value sooner by reducing operating friction, improving repair visibility, sharing assets, increasing resident power, and preparing buildings for retrofit. The pilot should prove service quality before claiming scale.

## 22. Success

PullUp succeeds when people have more security and less extraction.

### Resident outcomes

- lower Total Damage relative to comparable alternatives;
- fewer hidden or surprise costs;
- faster, clearer Fixes;
- fewer preventable displacements;
- greater trust, privacy control, and understanding;
- meaningful democratic participation without excessive time burden;
- improved accessibility, comfort, and belonging.

### Housing outcomes

- more homes permanently removed from speculation;
- stronger reserves and maintenance;
- shorter vacancies without weaker screening rights;
- successful internal transfers through Switch Up;
- fewer emergency repairs;
- more shared asset use.

### Climate outcomes

- lower energy and water use without behavioural punishment;
- lower resident energy burden;
- fewer leaks and avoidable equipment failures;
- more repair, reuse, and shared access;
- measurable retrofit progress.

### System outcomes

- no revenue dependence on rent increases, evictions, ads, or data sales;
- no material disparities created by recommendations or workflows;
- local governance remains active as the network grows;
- open portability and credible exit remain possible.

## 23. Failure tests

PullUp is failing if:

- the app becomes easier while housing remains equally extractive;
- providers can buy visibility or priority;
- essential rights become subscription upgrades;
- AI quietly becomes the decision-maker;
- Day One grants broad access by default;
- social activity affects housing;
- sensors monitor private life;
- resident participation becomes unpaid administrative labour;
- public subsidy creates a future private sale;
- local groups lose power as the platform grows;
- the brand sounds human but the institution behaves like PropTech in a hoodie.

## 26. Final foundation statement

PullUp makes housing technology answer a different question.

Not: **How do we extract more value from every square foot?**

But: **How do we help more people find a good home, keep it, share what makes sense, govern what affects them, and leave a livable planet behind?**

That is the foundation.
