# PullUp User Roles

> People are more than permissions. Roles exist to protect their choices, rights, and responsibilities.

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Product and access baseline  
**Date:** September 20, 2026

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the people who use, support, govern, audit, and operate PullUp.

It connects three things that must stay aligned:

- the mission to make safe, stable, climate-ready homes easier to access and harder to exploit;
- the product experience each person needs;
- the access boundaries the system must enforce.

This is a product and policy reference, not a substitute for legal tenancy, employment, safeguarding, or governance rules. A PullUp role can describe what someone may do in the platform. It cannot create, remove, or weaken a person’s rights outside it.

## 2. Role philosophy

### A role is a context, not a ranking

Roles describe why someone is here and what they need to do. They never describe someone’s worth, trustworthiness, vulnerability, or desirability.

One person may hold several roles. A resident might also be a resident leader, a Twin for a sibling, or an employee of another housing provider. PullUp keeps those contexts visibly separate and never lets one role silently unlock another.

### Resident power comes first

Operational convenience does not justify broad access to a person’s home, finances, application, relationships, or private life. Staff, partners, and operators receive the minimum access required for a specific responsibility.

### Access is more than a role name

Every protected action is evaluated using:

1. **identity**: who is acting;
2. **role**: what broad function they hold;
3. **relationship**: how they are connected to the person, Place, household, provider, or task;
4. **scope**: which exact record, building, case, or action is involved;
5. **purpose**: why access is needed;
6. **authority or consent**: what permits it;
7. **time**: when access starts, expires, or is revoked;
8. **assurance**: whether the sign-in method is strong enough;
9. **visibility**: what the affected person can see in Receipts or access history.

A role alone is never a master key.

### Housing stays separate from engagement

No role may use fLOKr activity, Day One use, popularity, volunteering, purchases, app engagement, or unrelated personal data to influence housing eligibility, price, repair priority, rights, or tenure.

### Human routes remain equal routes

Phone, paper, kiosk, in-person, translated, and supported journeys must not become slower or lower-priority versions of the app. A person who needs help is not a lesser user.

## 3. Role map

| Role | Why they use PullUp | Primary relationship | Default access posture |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Visitor** | Explore public Places and understand PullUp | None required | Public information only |
| **Applicant** | Tour, apply, and track a housing decision | Own application | Own records and deliberate shares |
| **Resident** | Run and protect life in a participating home | Place and provider | Own home, services, rights, and choices |
| **Household member** | Take part in selected Home Crew responsibilities | Household | Role-specific household access |
| **Twin** | Help one chosen person through Day One | Trusted personal relationship | Nothing until explicitly granted |
| **Resident leader** | Carry out elected or delegated governance work | Resident body or board | Governance scope only |
| **Community Lead** | Support approved community activity | fLOKr community scope | Community information only |
| **Provider worker** | Deliver assigned housing operations | Provider, building, or service | Job- and assignment-scoped access |
| **Repair worker** | Complete a selected Fix safely | Work order | Task-limited access |
| **Support partner** | Provide requested specialist help | Resident-approved case | Purpose- and time-limited access |
| **Auditor** | Test records, controls, and commitments | Approved audit mandate | Read-only evidence scope |
| **Platform operator** | Keep PullUp secure and available | Platform duty | No standing resident-content access |

## 4. Resident-side roles

### 4.1 Visitor

**Who they are**  
Anyone exploring PullUp before creating an account.

**What they need**

- browse public Places in Flow;
- view The Rundown, Total Damage, accessibility facts, provider identity, tenure, and major Heads Up items;
- understand PullUp’s ownership model, rights, privacy promises, and support routes;
- use list, map, keyboard, screen reader, print, phone, or human-assisted alternatives.

**Boundaries**

- Browsing public Places never requires an account.
- PullUp does not manufacture urgency, hide key facts behind sign-in, or treat a visitor as a sales lead.
- Public browsing does not create a housing or desirability profile.

**Tone**  
Welcoming, useful, and pressure-free. Lead with the home and the facts, not conversion language.

### 4.2 Applicant

**Who they are**  
A person using Home Passport, requesting a tour, applying for a Place, or tracking **Where We At?**

**What they need**

- control a reusable Home Passport;
- choose which fields and documents each provider receives;
- understand why every sensitive item is requested;
- request accessible or supported tours;
- see status, timing, responsible party, reasons, review, and appeal routes;
- view access history and end expired or unnecessary shares;
- continue through phone, paper, kiosk, or assisted service when needed.

**Boundaries**

- An application is not permission to inspect the applicant’s wider life.
- PullUp never creates a universal tenant, behaviour, vulnerability, or desirability score.
- Assisted applications enter the same process and priority rules as digital applications.
- Provider workers see only the information required for the selected application and their assigned task.

**Tone**  
Clear and reassuring without pretending the stakes are small. The applicant should feel informed, not judged or made to audition for a landlord.

### 4.3 Resident

**Who they are**  
A person who lives in a participating Place and holds resident rights under the relevant agreement, law, or cooperative structure.

**What they need**

- use My Place to understand charges, documents, notices, access, and upcoming decisions;
- report and track Fixes without retaliation;
- understand The Setup and emergency or offline fallbacks;
- manage Home Crew, Commons, Day One, My Business, and chosen notifications;
- use Damage Control, Stay Put, and Switch Up;
- participate in The Board and resident governance without social participation affecting housing;
- export their records and reach a qualified person when the app is not enough.

**Boundaries**

- Essential rights, repairs, safety, privacy, and due process are never premium features.
- A resident controls personal sharing but cannot use household controls to erase another person’s independent rights.
- Quiet participation is valid. Residents never owe visibility, volunteering, friendship, or app engagement in exchange for housing.

**Tone**  
Calm, direct, and respectful. Always show what happened, what comes next, who owns it, and when the resident should expect movement.

### 4.4 Household member

**Who they are**  
A person connected to a Home Crew. Their household role may be legal resident, roommate, caregiver, child or dependent, guest, or temporary support person.

**What they need**

- see the parts of household life relevant to their role;
- participate in selected Ground Rules, tasks, bookings, or shared resources;
- understand what they can access and why;
- leave or lose access cleanly when the relationship ends.

**Boundaries**

- Household membership is not automatically proof of tenancy, legal responsibility, or authority over another person.
- Payments, documents, messages, location, access credentials, and personal requests are not shared with the whole Home Crew by default.
- Children and dependants receive age-appropriate experiences and do not inherit adult permissions.
- Ground Rules cannot override law, provider duties, safety, or individual rights.

**Tone**  
Practical and neutral. Avoid family assumptions and support chosen, blended, multigenerational, caregiving, and shared-home arrangements.

### 4.5 Twin

**Who they are**  
One real person chosen through Day One, such as a friend, sibling, partner, parent, roommate, neighbour, advocate, or chosen family member.

**What they need**

- accept or decline a Twin Up invitation;
- use Twin Mode for only the records and tasks deliberately shared;
- help through moves such as Tag Team, Run It By Me, Hold It Down, Double Tap, Pull Through, and Our Move;
- understand every Max Pass purpose, scope, and expiry;
- see Receipts for their own actions.

**Boundaries**

- A Twin receives no access by default.
- PullUp does not assign, recommend, score, or infer a Twin.
- A Twin is not an AI copy, provider worker, emergency service, legal signer, or substitute decision-maker unless separate lawful authority exists.
- Revocation blocks new access immediately and invalidates active Max Pass credentials.
- Not using Day One never affects housing rights or service.

**Tone**  
Warm but explicit. Say exactly what is being shared, what the Twin may do, and when access ends. Trust should feel supported, never mined.

## 5. Community and governance roles

### 5.1 Resident leader

**Who they are**  
A resident holding an elected, rotating, or clearly delegated governance responsibility.

**What they need**

- prepare and review approved proposals, budgets, agendas, minutes, and decision records;
- support accessible participation in The Board;
- view the mandate, term, conflict declarations, and responsibilities attached to the role;
- hand work over cleanly when the term ends.

**Boundaries**

- Leadership does not unlock private applications, payments, repair details, support cases, Day One activity, or household records.
- A resident leader cannot waive protected rights or retaliate against disagreement.
- Conflicts of interest, abstentions, decisions, and reasons remain visible.
- The role expires with its elected or delegated term.

**Tone**  
Collective rather than managerial. Use language of stewardship, mandate, deliberation, reasons, and accountability.

### 5.2 Community Lead

**Who they are**  
A person trusted to support approved fLOKr activity, events, groups, or community moderation.

**What they need**

- organize approved community activity;
- apply transparent community guidelines;
- respond to reports through documented moderation and escalation routes;
- communicate decisions and review options.

**Boundaries**

- Community Lead activity cannot affect housing applications, price, repair priority, rights, or tenure.
- The role does not reveal Home Passport, payment, Fix, household, Day One, or private My Place information.
- No popularity scores, public tenant blacklists, crowd punishment, or informal surveillance.

**Tone**  
Neighbourly, fair, and non-performative. Community participation is an invitation, not rent paid in vibes.

## 6. Service and operational roles

### 6.1 Provider worker

**Who they are**  
A worker at an approved cooperative, nonprofit, municipal, public, community, Indigenous, or mission-locked housing partner.

Provider worker is a role family, not a universal staff account. Typical permission bundles may include Place publishing, tour coordination, application processing, resident service, repair coordination, finance operations, governance support, accessibility, or provider administration.

**What they need**

- complete assigned housing operations;
- see queues, deadlines, service levels, and accountable owners;
- communicate with applicants and residents;
- record reasons, corrections, handoffs, and decisions;
- escalate when policy, law, safety, or accessibility requires qualified review.

**Boundaries**

- Access is limited to the worker’s provider, buildings, job function, assignment, and current purpose.
- Staff status alone never authorizes browsing resident records.
- Consequential decisions require published rules, reasons, human review, and appeal information where available.
- Provider workers cannot use fLOKr participation, Day One activity, popularity, unrelated purchases, or private Makana information.
- Privileged roles use strong authentication and every override requires a reason.

**Tone**  
Task-first, factual, and accountable. Operator screens may be denser than resident screens, but never cryptic. Serious actions use plain language instead of playful slang.

### 6.2 Repair worker

**Who they are**  
An internal worker or approved contractor assigned to one or more Fixes.

**What they need**

- see the work order, location, schedule, relevant contact route, access method, safety information, and approved accommodations;
- update appointment, arrival, work, parts, follow-up, and completion status;
- report a newly discovered safety or repair issue.

**Boundaries**

- Repair workers do not receive application, payment, governance, Day One, or unrelated household information.
- Home access is specific, time-limited, revocable where possible, and backed by a physical or staffed fallback.
- Photos and notes stay limited to the repair purpose and never become informal surveillance.
- Workers cannot change repair priority based on a resident’s personality, complaint history, or perceived difficulty.

**Tone**  
Concrete and safety-first. Show the task, access conditions, accommodation, resident expectation, and next required update.

### 6.3 Support partner

**Who they are**  
An approved legal clinic, settlement worker, housing navigator, accessibility specialist, financial counsellor, stability worker, advocate, or other local helper.

**What they need**

- receive a resident-requested referral or support task;
- access the minimum selected information needed for that purpose;
- add updates, documents, appointments, or outcomes within the approved case scope;
- return control or close access when support ends.

**Boundaries**

- Access is purpose-specific, time-limited, auditable, and visible to the resident where lawful.
- A referral does not open the resident’s full housing file.
- Support partners do not make housing allocation or tenure decisions unless a separate lawful role explicitly requires it.
- Sensitive disclosures are never reused for marketing, profiling, unrelated casework, or model training.

**Tone**  
Respectful and non-stigmatizing. Describe the help, not a label placed on the person.

### 6.4 Auditor

**Who they are**  
An approved internal, resident-appointed, regulatory, financial, privacy, accessibility, security, or mission auditor.

**What they need**

- inspect the records and controls named in an audit mandate;
- test whether decisions, access, money, service, and governance match PullUp’s commitments;
- export approved evidence and record findings;
- verify remediation without modifying the original record.

**Boundaries**

- Audit access is read-only unless a separate remediation role is assigned.
- Use aggregated, redacted, or pseudonymized evidence whenever individual identity is unnecessary.
- The mandate defines provider, system, data class, purpose, and time window.
- Auditors cannot quietly expand scope or use evidence for unrelated enforcement, profiling, or commercial purposes.

**Tone**  
Precise, evidence-led, and unambiguous. Warmth never replaces a clear finding, owner, deadline, or unresolved risk.

### 6.5 Platform operator

**Who they are**  
A Sakia Labs or authorized PullUp worker responsible for platform reliability, security, account support, incident response, and approved configuration.

**What they need**

- monitor technical health without collecting unnecessary resident content;
- support account recovery and provider configuration;
- investigate security, privacy, availability, and data-integrity incidents;
- use narrow break-glass access when normal tools cannot resolve a serious issue.

**Boundaries**

- Platform operators have no standing reason to read resident applications, documents, messages, household details, or private notes.
- Privileged actions require strong authentication, a stated reason, narrow scope, short duration, immutable logging, and after-action review.
- Operators cannot make housing, repair, governance, or support decisions merely because they can administer the software.
- Production data is not a casual debugging dataset.

**Tone**  
Calm, transparent, and accountable. Incident communication states what happened, who is affected, what is known, what is being done, and when the next update will arrive.

## 7. Role transitions and combinations

### Common transitions

- **Visitor → Applicant:** occurs only when the person chooses to begin a tour or application journey.
- **Applicant → Resident:** adds a Place relationship after the appropriate agreement or membership becomes active. It does not erase application history or consent records.
- **Resident → Former resident:** removes active home access while preserving lawful records, export rights, open disputes, and required history.
- **Resident → Resident leader:** adds a separate, expiring governance mandate.
- **Person → Twin:** requires both people to accept Twin Up; all useful access still comes through Max Pass.
- **Worker → Former worker:** ends active sessions, assignments, keys, and privileged grants immediately while preserving audit records.

### Multiple roles

When someone holds more than one role:

- the interface shows the active context clearly;
- switching context is deliberate, never inferred from the page being viewed;
- permissions do not merge into one larger invisible role;
- audit records capture which role and purpose were used;
- conflicts of interest are declared and handled;
- the least-privileged valid role is used for the action.

A resident who also works for a provider sees their own home as a resident and performs assigned work in a visibly separate worker context.

## 8. Language and tone by role

| Audience | Voice | Always include | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Visitors | Welcoming and pressure-free | Material facts and plain next steps | Leads, conversion, scarcity theatre |
| Applicants | Reassuring and exact | Purpose, status, timing, reasons, review | Worthiness language or opaque scores |
| Residents | Calm and respectful | Rights, owner, deadline, support route | Blame, retaliation, forced cheerfulness |
| Household members | Practical and inclusive | Role, visibility, consent, expiry | Assumptions about family or authority |
| Twins | Warm and explicit | Scope, action, purpose, expiry, Receipts | “Full access,” implied monitoring |
| Resident leaders | Collective and accountable | Mandate, conflict, record, review | Corporate command-and-control language |
| Provider and repair workers | Direct and task-focused | Assignment, standard, reason, due time | Slang for serious actions or resident labels |
| Support partners | Respectful and non-stigmatizing | Requested help, minimum disclosure, closure | Defining a person by a crisis |
| Auditors and operators | Precise and evidence-led | Scope, source, owner, deadline, audit trail | Vague reassurance or invisible overrides |

### Preferred role language

Use specific labels such as **resident**, **applicant**, **household member**, **Twin**, **resident leader**, **provider worker**, **repair worker**, **support partner**, **auditor**, and **platform operator**.

Avoid labels such as **good tenant**, **bad tenant**, **problem resident**, **vulnerable user**, **lead**, **prospect**, **high-value resident**, **community score**, or a vague **admin** role with unlimited power.

## 9. Implementation contract

### Stable role identifiers

Use stable internal identifiers while localizing display labels:

```text
visitor
applicant
resident
household_member
twin
resident_leader
community_lead
provider_worker
repair_worker
support_partner
auditor
platform_operator
```

### Permissions, not role explosions

Broad roles should be combined with scoped permissions such as:

```text
places.publish
tours.coordinate
applications.review
residents.support
fixes.assign
fixes.complete
commons.manage
governance.prepare
payments.reconcile
access.issue_temporary
audit.read
platform.recover_account
```

Do not model privileged access as a universal `is_admin` switch. Provider administration, platform operation, auditing, and resident governance are different powers and must remain separate.

### Every grant records

- actor and active role;
- relationship or assignment;
- provider, building, Place, household, case, task, or record scope;
- allowed action;
- purpose and authority;
- start, expiry, and revocation;
- assurance requirement;
- visibility and audit treatment.

### Role-switching interface

- Show the active role and organization or household context.
- Require an explicit switch before entering a privileged workspace.
- Never mix a worker queue with the worker’s personal resident information.
- Confirm the active context again before consequential actions.
- End privileged context after inactivity and show active sessions in My Business.

## 10. Identities that are not user roles

- **Housing provider:** an organization and authorization boundary, not one human account.
- **Building device:** an enrolled technical identity with a narrow purpose, never a resident or staff role.
- **Integration or service account:** a machine identity with explicit API scope, expiry, and rotation.
- **AI assistant:** a supporting capability that may explain, translate, summarize, route, or recommend. It does not hold authority, consent, a vote, or responsibility for a housing decision.
- **Emergency service:** an external service reached through a clear escalation route, not simulated by PullUp.

## 11. Explicitly excluded roles

PullUp does not create roles for:

- advertisers buying resident attention or data;
- brokers purchasing ranking or access;
- speculative landlords seeking lead generation without mission obligations;
- tenant scorers or behaviour-ranking vendors;
- unverified contractors browsing open work or resident information;
- AI decision-makers approving, rejecting, penalizing, or evicting people;
- universal super-admins with invisible, permanent access.

## 12. Acceptance checklist

Before a role or permission ships:

- [ ] The person can understand why the role exists.
- [ ] Every allowed action has a clear scope and purpose.
- [ ] Sensitive access has an authority, consent, or lawful basis.
- [ ] The role has an explicit deny list, not only an allow list.
- [ ] Access expires or can be revoked where appropriate.
- [ ] The affected person can see access history where lawful.
- [ ] Privileged actions record actor, role, reason, time, and result.
- [ ] Role use cannot influence housing through prohibited social or behavioural data.
- [ ] Mobile, accessible, translated, offline, and human-assisted routes are accounted for.
- [ ] Role changes remove stale sessions, assignments, credentials, and grants.
- [ ] A person holding multiple roles sees and chooses the active context.
- [ ] The language respects the person and remains plain around serious decisions.
