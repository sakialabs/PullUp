# PullUp Product

> See the place. Know the damage. PullUp.

**Version:** 1.1  
**Status:** Product definition baseline  
**Depends on:** [foundation.md](../project/foundation.md)  
**Date:** August 2026

## 1. Product thesis

PullUp is the housing layer of the Sakia Labs ecosystem.

It helps everyday people:

1. find a suitable Place;
2. see the full cost and honest tradeoffs;
3. apply without repeating the same paperwork;
4. use Day One to plan and act with one chosen Twin;
5. move in with a clear plan;
6. manage repairs, access, payments, shared resources, and household life;
7. participate in local decisions without living in meetings;
8. stay housed when life changes;
9. share useful assets and clean energy;
10. keep control of their data.

The interaction can feel light. The material facts cannot be light.

## 2. Product promise

**Find your place. PullUp.**

PullUp gives people a welcoming front door to homes that are clear about cost, tenure, accessibility, condition, governance, and community.

It gives public, cooperative, nonprofit, and mission-locked providers a shared operating system that improves service without becoming a new corporate landlord.

## 3. Who it is for

### People looking for a home

Renters, cooperative members, newcomers, workers, students, families, older adults, disabled people, people leaving unsafe or unstable situations, remote workers, and nomads looking for a stable landing.

### Current residents

People who need repairs, household coordination, understandable costs, shared resources, energy savings, transfers, housing-stability support, and real participation.

### Trusted humans

Friends, siblings, partners, parents, roommates, neighbours, advocates, and chosen family who can become a Twin through Day One with narrow permission.

### Housing partners

Housing co-ops, nonprofits, municipalities, public providers, community land trusts, Indigenous housing partners where invited, mutual-aid groups, and mission-locked developers.

### Local helpers

Legal clinics, settlement workers, housing navigators, accessibility experts, repair teams, energy advisors, community organizers, and technical-assistance organizations.

## 5. The full resident journey

## 5.1 Start with needs, not a personality quiz

A person can begin anonymously or with a minimal account.

They choose practical needs such as:

- location or flexible area;
- target Total Damage;
- move date;
- household size;
- minimum tenure;
- accessibility;
- pets;
- transit;
- work or school travel;
- quiet, social, family, or mixed environment;
- shared-space comfort;
- climate and comfort needs;
- private home, room, cooperative, or flexible landing.

PullUp does not infer protected traits, vulnerability, income class, or “desirability” from behaviour.

### Simple analogy

It should feel like telling a thoughtful friend what kind of home works, not auditioning for a landlord.

## 5.2 Browse the Flow

Flow presents Places through optional cards, list, and map views.

Every Place card shows material facts before lifestyle marketing:

- Total Damage;
- what is included;
- tenure type and expected term;
- next availability;
- accessibility facts;
- room and household type;
- approximate transport context;
- major Heads Up items;
- provider and ownership type;
- verified status;
- representative photos.

### Optional gestures

- swipe right to **Keep**;
- swipe left for **Not It**;
- swipe up to **Pull Up** for a tour.

### Equal controls

The same actions are always available as visible buttons. List, map, keyboard, screen reader, voice, phone, print, kiosk, and assisted flows receive the same information and outcomes.

No one must swipe.

## 5.3 Open The Rundown

The Rundown is the complete profile of a Place.

### Total Damage

The full expected monthly and move-in cost, not a bait number.

It may include:

- housing charge or rent;
- utilities and typical range;
- internet;
- required membership charges;
- parking or mobility;
- laundry;
- insurance estimate;
- deposits or member contribution;
- furniture or equipment;
- expected one-time move costs;
- optional services clearly separated.

### Where It Goes

A plain breakdown of each charge, who receives it, what it funds, whether it can change, and how changes are governed.

### The Vibe

Practical living character, not a popularity score:

- quiet or active;
- family mix;
- pets;
- shared meals or none;
- private and shared-space balance;
- common activities;
- smoking rules;
- sensory environment;
- household practices;
- participation options.

The Vibe never uses protected traits as ranking signals.

### Heads Up

Honest tradeoffs such as:

- stairs or limited elevator reliability;
- street noise;
- construction nearby;
- small kitchen;
- limited storage;
- fragrance-free expectations;
- shared bathroom;
- long transit to a specific area;
- planned renovation;
- unusual utility variation.

A charming interface earns trust by telling the awkward truth.

### Word on the Block

Verified resident insight about living conditions and provider service.

Safeguards:

- proof of resident relationship;
- privacy-preserving display;
- structured topics plus open comment;
- moderation against harassment and discrimination;
- no retaliation;
- provider right to respond;
- no resident popularity scores.

### The Backstory

Relevant building history:

- major repairs;
- accessibility upgrades;
- energy retrofit;
- inspections;
- recurring issues;
- pest treatment;
- planned work;
- equipment age where useful.

Private resident information never appears.

### Around Here

Useful nearby context:

- transit;
- groceries;
- healthcare;
- schools and childcare;
- libraries;
- parks;
- community services;
- work hubs;
- cycling and walking access;
- common travel times;
- climate risks and cooling access.

### Fine Print

Lexi provides source-backed plain-language explanations of agreements, policies, deadlines, rights, and responsibilities. Original documents remain available. People can ask a qualified human for legal help.

## 5.4 Save and compare

### Keepers

Saved Places remain private unless the user shares them.

Users can add personal notes, labels, and questions.

### Side by Side

Comparison focuses on meaningful differences:

- Total Damage;
- move-in cost;
- tenure security;
- accessibility;
- space;
- transport;
- energy and comfort;
- Heads Up items;
- household fit;
- provider model;
- available Commons.

Side by Side does not crown a universal “best” Place. Fit belongs to the person.

## 5.5 Turn on Day One

The user can turn on **Day One** and **Twin Up** with one chosen human.

A Twin may be allowed to:

- view selected Keepers;
- add comparison notes;
- help prepare tour questions;
- join a tour;
- review selected Home Passport fields;
- see chosen deadlines;
- help with a Move Plan;
- Tag Team on selected tasks;
- receive a Run It By Me request;
- Hold It Down for a specific task and time;
- Double Tap a sensitive action;
- Split It through an approved TapIn flow;
- respond when the user asks them to Pull Through.

The user selects each permission. A **Max Pass** can expire after a tour, application, move, task, or chosen time.

Day One never lets the Twin automatically:

- see all Places;
- view income or identity documents;
- access payments;
- unlock doors;
- read messages;
- view Makana reflections;
- act as the user.

## 5.6 Pull Up for a tour

People can request:

- in-person tour;
- live video tour;
- accessible tour;
- group open house;
- trusted-person tour;
- recorded walkthrough with freshness date;
- phone-based description;
- reasonable accommodation.

Tour information includes:

- who will attend;
- identity and role of host;
- accessibility details;
- what documents are not required;
- how to report an unsafe or discriminatory interaction;
- whether a Twin may join;
- directions through Around Here.

TapIn may provide time-limited tour access. It never creates a permanent key or default access for a Twin.

## 5.7 Apply once with Home Passport

Home Passport is a reusable, resident-controlled application.

Possible sections:

- contact information;
- household information;
- housing needs;
- income or eligibility evidence where lawfully required;
- references where lawful and necessary;
- accessibility requests;
- pets;
- selected documents;
- consent and sharing history.

### Core rules

- collect only what is necessary;
- explain why each item is requested;
- share a selected packet, not the whole account;
- support secure verification without exposing unnecessary originals;
- show who accessed what and when;
- allow correction and expiry;
- support offline and assisted applications;
- never create a universal tenant score.

### Fair allocation

Provider rules are explicit and reviewable.

Depending on law and program, allocation may use:

- eligibility bands;
- chronological or randomized selection;
- household and unit fit;
- accessibility priority;
- urgent or rights-based priority;
- community-approved public rules.

It does not use app engagement, attractiveness, social connections, purchasing behaviour, or secret AI ranking.

## 5.8 Track Where We At?

Where We At? shows:

- submitted;
- received;
- information needed;
- verification in progress;
- eligible;
- selected;
- waitlisted;
- offer made;
- declined;
- withdrawn;
- appeal or review available.

Every status has:

- timestamp;
- next step;
- responsible party;
- expected timing where possible;
- reason for material decisions;
- help and appeal route.

No ghosting as a product strategy.

## 5.9 Review, sign, and pay

### Fine Print with Lexi

PullUp presents:

- original agreement;
- plain-language explanation;
- material changes;
- key dates;
- duties;
- cost changes;
- exit and transfer terms;
- rights and support sources.

Lexi explains. A human makes legal judgments.

### TapIn

TapIn handles approved:

- member contributions;
- deposits where lawful;
- housing charges;
- optional Commons bookings;
- payment plans;
- refunds;
- shared household costs;
- receipts and audit trail.

Total Damage is visible before payment. Optional charges are separate. No preselected add-ons.

## 5.10 Follow the Move Plan

Move Plan is a private, editable journey from accepted offer to settled home.

It may include:

- agreement signed;
- payment schedule;
- utility changes;
- accessibility preparation;
- move date;
- address updates;
- key or access setup;
- Home Crew invitations;
- inspections;
- furniture and Commons bookings;
- transit and service changes;
- important contacts;
- first-week Fixes;
- Day One tasks in Our Move.

Makana can support the emotional and practical transition. Private reflections remain outside PullUp decision systems.

## 5.11 Run My Place

My Place is the resident dashboard.

It presents:

- next housing charge;
- Total Damage history;
- open Fixes;
- notices;
- bookings;
- household;
- access;
- documents;
- upcoming decisions;
- energy and comfort information;
- privacy controls;
- support.

The default view is calm and useful, not a casino of badges.

## 5.12 Understand The Setup

The Setup is the practical map of the home.

It includes:

- rooms and fixtures;
- appliances;
- heating and cooling;
- water shutoff;
- electrical panel guidance;
- ventilation;
- accessibility controls;
- shared systems;
- manuals;
- warranties;
- service dates;
- maintenance guidance;
- emergency instructions.

Physical QR or NFC Fix Tags may open the correct item and repair form. A printed binder or phone support remains available.

## 5.13 Report Fixes

Fixes turns repair requests into accountable work.

Residents can:

- describe the problem in text, photo, voice, or phone;
- identify the affected Setup item;
- mark urgent health or safety issues;
- request accommodation;
- choose safe entry windows;
- see assigned team and timing;
- receive updates;
- confirm completion;
- reopen unresolved work;
- escalate or appeal.

### Provider tools

Partners receive:

- triage;
- work orders;
- inventory;
- contractor coordination;
- preventive schedules;
- recurring-issue detection;
- accessibility flags with limited visibility;
- resident communication;
- service-level tracking;
- reserve planning.

AI may help route and summarize. It cannot downgrade a repair because a resident is considered “difficult.”

## 5.14 Coordinate the Home Crew

Home Crew is the household.

Members can have distinct roles:

- legal resident;
- household member;
- caregiver;
- child or dependent;
- roommate;
- guest;
- temporary support person.

Permissions follow role and consent. Household visibility is not all-or-nothing.

### Ground Rules

Households can record practical expectations such as:

- quiet hours;
- cleaning;
- guests;
- shared food;
- accessibility needs;
- pets;
- bills;
- shared items;
- conflict process.

Ground Rules are household agreements, not a substitute for law or building policy.

## 5.15 Use the Commons

Commons is the library-economy layer.

Residents may browse, reserve, borrow, or propose:

- tools;
- cargo bikes;
- mobility devices;
- guest rooms;
- work rooms;
- studios;
- event space;
- storage;
- kitchen equipment;
- garden space;
- games and media;
- accessibility equipment;
- workshops and skills;
- local energy capacity where supported.

Each resource shows:

- availability;
- access needs;
- actual incremental cost;
- deposit if justified;
- care instructions;
- accessibility;
- repair status;
- community rules.

TapIn handles approved booking and access. fLOKr can host related Signals, Crews, Shifts, and Moves.

## 5.16 Follow The Board

The Board is the building’s clear public notice space.

It includes:

- maintenance and outages;
- planned work;
- emergencies;
- meetings;
- proposals;
- budgets;
- decisions;
- events;
- new Commons;
- service changes.

People can follow only the topics they want. Critical notices use more than push notifications and remain available by phone, email, print, display, and accessible formats.

## 5.17 Use Damage Control

Damage Control helps before a missed payment becomes a crisis.

It may connect people to:

- transparent payment plans;
- benefits screening;
- emergency assistance;
- income support;
- utility relief;
- financial counselling;
- legal or tenant support;
- trusted community organizations.

The feature requires consent and minimal disclosure. It is not a debt-collection funnel or vulnerability score.

## 5.18 Stay Put

Stay Put is the housing-stability pathway.

It supports:

- early, voluntary help;
- reasonable payment plans;
- benefit and legal referrals;
- accessibility accommodation;
- conflict support;
- repair and habitability escalation;
- safety planning with qualified partners;
- human case review;
- clear due process.

PullUp does not automate eviction recommendations. The goal is to prevent avoidable displacement while respecting safety and law.

## 5.19 Switch Up

Switch Up helps people move within the PullUp network when a home no longer fits.

Reasons may include:

- household change;
- accessibility;
- affordability;
- work or school;
- caregiving;
- safety;
- too much or too little space;
- relocation;
- building renovation.

Home Passport and selected history can travel with the person. They should not start from zero because life changed.

## 5.20 Control My Business

My Business is the privacy and permission centre.

Residents can:

- review stored information;
- correct data;
- see access history;
- manage Home Passport shares;
- manage Day One access;
- control selected cross-platform connections;
- set expiry;
- download records;
- close optional services;
- ask for human help.

The interface uses real explanations, not “we value your privacy” followed by forty toggles in legal fog.

## 6. fLOKr connection

| fLOKr term | PullUp use |
|---|---|
| **Loop** | Local feed selected by the resident |
| **Signals** | Asks, offers, updates, and useful notices |
| **Circles** | Interest, building, neighbourhood, or support communities |
| **Crews** | Small teams doing practical work |
| **Leads** | Trusted human coordinators or helpers |
| **Shifts** | Paid work, skill-sharing, or scheduled support |

Examples:

- a repair workshop is a Move;
- a garden group is a Circle;
- neighbours requesting a safer crossing post a Signal and form a Crew;

### The wall between community and housing decisions

PullUp does not send social participation, likes, attendance, popularity, or community conflict into eligibility, pricing, repairs, or tenure.

People can find their fLOKr. They do not have to perform belonging.

## 10. Day One across Sakia Labs

Day One is the Sakia-wide human-support layer. The **Twin** is the one chosen human inside it.

| Platform | A Twin may help with through Day One | Never shared by default |
|---|---|---|
| **PullUp** | Keepers, Side by Side, tours, selected Home Passport items, Our Move, and selected Fixes | Full identity, all documents, private messages |
| **fLOKr** | Put Me On to selected Signals, Circles, Crews, Leads, Shifts, and Moves | Complete social activity |
| **TapIn** | Double Tap, Split It, approved bookings, and Max Pass access | General money history, permanent keys |
| **Lexi** | Run It By Me on selected documents, deadlines, and question lists | All legal or personal questions |
| **Makana** | Chosen Check Ins, Our Move goals, and celebrations | Private reflections |

### Day One interaction model

- **Twin Up** creates the relationship.
- **Twin Mode** opens a shared working view.
- **Tag Team** assigns selected tasks.
- **Run It By Me** asks for a second opinion.
- **Put Me On** shares a selected Place, person, Circle, Lead, Shift, or Move.
- **Hold It Down** grants temporary authority over a specific task.
- **Double Tap** adds optional two-person confirmation.
- **Split It** divides an approved cost without exposing full accounts.
- **Pull Through** sends an urgent practical request.
- **Check In** shares one chosen signal.
- **Our Move** holds the shared plan.
- **Max Pass** defines scope and expiry.
- **Receipts** shows access and action history.

Day One is a core capability, not a paid upgrade. The relationship is created by the user, not predicted by AI. It can later support an opt-in roommate relationship, but roommate matching is a separate product problem.

## 11. Roommate and shared-home principles

PullUp may eventually help people find compatible shared homes.

It must:

- be opt-in and mutual;
- focus on living practices, budget, accessibility, schedule, pets, guests, substances, noise, care needs, and boundaries;
- reveal only selected information;
- give people reasons and controls;
- support safety and reporting;
- avoid attractiveness ranking;
- avoid protected-trait inference;
- avoid a universal compatibility score;
- never turn Day One into an assigned stranger.

People may discover a potential roommate. They choose whether trust grows. The product cannot manufacture intimacy.

## 12. Quiet intelligence

PullUp does not need an obnoxious helper orbiting every screen.

**Need a Hand?** opens the appropriate help:

- clear explanation;
- guided form;
- translated content;
- search;
- contextual suggestion;
- Day One prompt;
- live worker;
- emergency or legal referral.

AI and data science stay under the hood.

### Good uses

- rank Places by user-selected fit;
- summarize differences;
- translate;
- extract structured facts from provider documents with review;
- detect missing or conflicting listing data;
- route Fixes;
- forecast parts and maintenance demand;
- detect leaks or equipment anomalies;
- optimize shared energy;
- estimate queues;
- monitor service disparities.

### Prohibited uses

- automated rejection;
- tenant, behaviour, or vulnerability scoring;
- eviction recommendation;
- dynamic housing charges;
- price discrimination;
- protected-trait inference;
- face, gait, emotion, or voice analysis;
- social participation scoring;
- deceptive urgency;
- hidden provider-paid ranking.

### Consequential-decision standard

If a system materially affects access, cost, rights, service, or tenure:

1. the rule is documented;
2. the data is relevant and minimized;
3. the person receives a reason;
4. a qualified human can review;
5. an appeal path exists;
6. disparity and error are measured;
7. the feature can be paused when unsafe.

## 13. IoT and future home gadgets

Hardware remains a supporting layer, not the product’s personality.

### Useful concepts

- leak and flood alerts;
- air quality, humidity, and temperature;
- heating, cooling, solar, battery, and charging controls;
- equipment-health monitoring;
- accessible switches and alerts;
- Fix Tags for the right manual or repair form;
- secure temporary access;
- shared lockers;
- Commons availability;
- common-area information displays.

### Resident experience

The Setup explains every device:

- what it senses;
- why it exists;
- who sees the data;
- how long data is kept;
- whether it can be disabled;
- what happens offline;
- how to report a problem.

### Hard limits

No cameras or microphones inside homes. No facial, voice, gait, or emotion recognition. No private-room occupancy inference. No sensor-based discipline, pricing, or eviction. No sale of device data.

## 14. Accessibility

PullUp is designed for:

- screen readers;
- keyboard-only navigation;
- switch, voice, and alternative pointer access;
- zoom and large text;
- reduced motion;
- high contrast;
- captions and transcripts;
- plain language;
- translation;
- cognitive accessibility;
- low bandwidth;
- older devices;
- phone and paper;
- assisted use;
- disability-related accommodation without unnecessary disclosure.

Swipe interactions always have single-pointer and non-gesture alternatives, consistent with WCAG 2.2.

Accessibility facts about Places must be specific. “Accessible” alone is not useful. A Place should describe entrances, doors, lifts, bathrooms, counters, controls, sensory conditions, evacuation, parking, and routes.

## 18. Notifications

Notifications are grouped by urgency:

### Critical

Safety, access, severe outage, urgent repair, legal deadline, or immediate displacement risk.

### Action needed

Application item, signature, payment choice, tour, governance deadline, or repair appointment.

### Update

Status change, completed Fix, Commons availability, building work, or Day One activity.

### Community

Selected fLOKr and Board activity.

Users control channels and quiet hours. Critical information uses redundant channels. The product does not use streaks, fake countdowns, or engagement panic.

## 19. Product metrics

### North-star outcome

**Stable, suitable homes with less extraction and more resident control.**

- percentage of Places with verified Total Damage;
- time to find a suitable Place;
- accessibility-information completeness;
- tour-to-application rate by user choice, not pressure;
- scam and duplicate rate.

- application completion burden;
- repeated document requests;
- decision time;
- reasons delivered;
- appeal availability and outcomes;
- disparities by lawful audit categories.

### Resident service

- repeat repair rate;
- safety response;
- resident-rated clarity and respect;
- Stay Put success;
- Switch Up success.

- surprise cost rate;
- arrears resolved without displacement;
- Commons savings;
- procurement savings;
- reserve health.

### Democracy

- participation across channels;
- representation gaps;
- proposal completion;
- decision comprehension;
- paid resident work;
- time burden.

### Climate

- energy burden;
- emissions and consumption;
- leak prevention;
- equipment failure reduction;
- repair and reuse;
- shared asset utilization.

### Trust

- consent comprehension;
- privacy incidents;
- permission revocation success;
- data-access requests;
- recommendation disparities;
- complaints and resolution.

Vanity metrics such as daily swipes, notification opens, or screen time are never the definition of success.

## 20. Pilot product boundary

The first pilot should prove a small, complete loop.

### Include

- partner and Place onboarding;
- The Rundown;
- Total Damage and Where It Goes;
- Flow with list and accessible alternatives;
- Keepers and Side by Side;
- tour requests;
- lightweight Home Passport;
- Where We At?;
- My Place;
- Fixes;
- The Board;
- a small Commons catalogue;
- My Business;
- selected fLOKr connections;
- Day One for selected tasks;
- one consent-based IoT use case;
- resident and provider outcome measurement.

### Defer until proven

- broad public marketplace;
- automated allocation;
- roommate matching;
- dynamic cross-city membership;
- advanced energy trading;
- large hardware rollout;
- speculative consumer credit;
- public social reputation;
- autonomous legal or housing decisions.

The pilot is not the roadmap. It is the product boundary the [roadmap](../project/roadmap.md) must respect.

## 21. Experience principles for future design

1. **Warm before clinical.**
2. **Facts before fantasy.**
3. **Total Damage before teaser price.**
4. **Place fit before popularity.**
5. **Buttons as equal citizens, not swipe leftovers.**
6. **One calm next step.**
7. **Human help without an AI mascot.**
8. **Serious actions in serious language.**
9. **Receipts for every consequential action.**
10. **Humour points up at extraction, never down at people.**

## 24. Product statement

PullUp is what happens when the ease of a great consumer app answers to the politics of a housing cooperative, the permanence of a community land trust, the usefulness of a library, and the optimism of solarpunk.

It helps people find their place, know the damage, bring their people, move with less chaos, run home life with dignity, and keep housing accountable after the keys change hands.
